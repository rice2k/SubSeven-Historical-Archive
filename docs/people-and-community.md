# Developers, attribution, and the Sub7 community

SubSeven history is unusually easy to misstate because the name **Mobman** was later claimed by more than one person, community members produced add-ons and later continuations, and many surviving pages are mirrors rather than primary records. This archive separates verified attribution from community participation and later preservation work.

## Mobman — original programmer handle

Every classic SubSeven generation credits a programmer using the handle **Mobman**. For many years, public retellings attached that handle to conflicting identities.

In 2024, Darknet Diaries revisited an earlier interview after researcher **Ill Will** traced a different person who used the Mobman handle during the original SubSeven era. The episode reports that the newly contacted Mobman described SubSeven as his own Delphi project, said he had retained the original source, and discussed the Romanian B.U.G. Mafia references embedded throughout the program. Darknet Diaries ultimately concluded that its earlier interview subject, Greg, was not the original SubSeven author.

For historical accuracy this repository therefore uses **Mobman** as the classic programmer's handle and avoids treating older "Greg = Mobman" articles as settled attribution.

### What the creator said about development

The 2024 interview is especially useful because the original programmer describes the development process at a high level:

- SubSeven was inspired in part by earlier remote-access trojans such as **NetBus**.
- The creator said SubSeven was his first substantial programming project.
- He said he learned **Delphi** while building it and described the source as messy/spaghetti code.
- He said he retained control of the source through the original development period rather than running an open collaborative source project.
- Feature requests arrived as the program became more popular, contributing to its rapid early release cycle.

These comments help explain why the 1999 release history contains many closely spaced point releases and why exact early package/source records are difficult to reconstruct today.

## Ill Will

**Ill Will** is important to the modern historical record as a researcher/preservation figure. Darknet Diaries credits his multi-year research with helping locate the person it later accepted as the original Mobman. A GitLab repository under the IllWill name also preserves a source-code record associated with SubSeven 2.1.3.

This archive links that repository for provenance but does not mirror weaponizable classic source code.

## Read101

**Read101** appears in later Sub7 community history and is associated with work after the original author's main development period. The 2024 Darknet Diaries conversation specifically distinguishes later Read101 involvement from the original sequence of releases authored by Mobman. Because surviving secondary pages sometimes blur these periods, this archive treats Read101 as a **later community/continuation contributor**, not as co-author of the original 1999–2003 line unless a specific release is supported by primary evidence.

## Sub7 Crew

The **Sub7 Crew** became a major community identity around the project. Its historical web footprint is preserved especially well in the user's Google Drive URL crawls for `sub7crew.org`.

Recovered paths show that the site was more than a download page. It hosted or linked:

- project/news pages;
- help and reference material;
- an official SubSeven mailing-list archive endpoint;
- IRC pages and IRC-bot material;
- a UBB-style forum;
- a gallery;
- plugin-related navigation/assets;
- member sub-sites;
- historical utility/add-on pages;
- removal/defensive material;
- download/index pages and release-name records.

### Handles/member areas preserved by the URL corpus

The Drive crawl contains member-directory records for handles including:

- `azzazzin`
- `fc`
- `mistahq`
- `qroject`
- `chezz`

These paths establish community presence and site ownership areas, but a URL alone does not prove authorship of a specific SubSeven release. Each person's role should be attributed only when a primary page, credit screen, README, mailing-list post, or other period evidence supports it.

## DarkCoderSc / Jean-Pierre Lesueur — SubSeven Legacy

The modern **SubSeven Legacy** project is authored by **Jean-Pierre Lesueur (DarkCoderSc)** and hosted in the official `DarkCoderSc/SubSeven` GitHub repository. It is explicitly described as a Delphi remake intended to preserve the look/feel of SubSeven while omitting malicious features.

The repository is structurally separate from classic SubSeven and contains modern projects for:

- Viewer
- Service
- Tray controller
- Helper
- Certificate generator
- Secure Desktop component
- Shared code/resources
- Installer definitions
- x86/x64 runtime dependencies

Legacy should therefore be documented as a **historical recreation**, not as the recovered original 1999 source tree.

## Attribution rules used by this archive

To avoid repeating long-lived SubSeven myths, this repository uses the following evidence order:

1. Original credit screens, READMEs, source comments, mailing-list posts, and dated website snapshots.
2. Authenticated/traceable source repositories and direct interviews with participants.
3. Period security advisories and contemporaneous technical documentation.
4. Later archival catalogs and high-quality historical reporting.
5. Forum posts, mirrors, fan pages, and unsourced claims only as leads.

When evidence conflicts, the conflict is recorded rather than silently resolved.
